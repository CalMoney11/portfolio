Three projects in ArcGIS Pro. For each one I drew the workflow as a flowchart in Eraser.io before touching the data, so I knew which layers I needed and could trace my steps back when something looked wrong.

Housing insecurity in Lane County, Oregon

ArcGIS Pro, U.S. Census data, Eraser.io

Where do renters pay more than 30% of their income on housing?

I pulled census data, joined it to a map of the county, and broke it down by area. The answer was right around campus. Landlords know students need housing near the university, so rents go up, and students end up paying more than they can afford. It hit close to home because it's data about the neighborhood I live in.

  1. Start with the wider region

    The dataset covered far more than Lane County, so I first mapped urban areas across the Willamette Valley to set the context. Along the way I learned that CBSA stands for Core Based Statistical Area, a unit used across a lot of census analysis.

  2. List every layer I'd need

    This flowchart made sure I had all the layers and data before I started building the map.

  3. Write down the processing steps

    Cutting a large dataset down to something useful is where it's easy to lose important information. This one is my roadmap for that part.

  4. The final map

    Most of the housing insecurity in the county is concentrated around the University of Oregon.

Buildable land near parks and trails in Austin

ArcGIS Pro, Eraser.io

If I worked in the City of Austin planning office, which open parcels would make the best home sites?

The brief was to find parcels inside the city limits that are available for development and close to parks and trails.

  1. Plan it as a flowchart

    A full flowchart keeps me organized while I build, and gives me something to check against when a result looks off.

  2. Load the data

    Parks, trails, city limits and developable land, each as its own layer on the main map.

  3. Buffer and overlap

    I buffered the trails and parks, then checked where those buffers overlap developable land.

What I learned

  • Using Buffer, Clip and Select by Location together to narrow a question down.
  • Handling and cleaning spatial data inside ArcGIS Pro.
  • Ended with a short list of parcels close to both parks and trails.

Comparing map projections

ArcGIS Pro

Starting from data my professor provided, I reprojected the same map into a set of common and uncommon projections and laid them out side by side so the distortion is easy to compare. It was also my first layout in ArcGIS Pro. The data is in project_1.zip on my GitHub.

What I learned

  • What common and uncommon projections are each good for.
  • How to build and adjust a layout.
  • Getting around the ArcGIS Pro tools and interface.
